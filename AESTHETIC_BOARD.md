# Chronosword's Last Day — aesthetic board

This is a production board, not a bag of moods. Every reference below has a
traceable source and an explicit job in the game. None of the photographs need
to be shipped with the build. They are for composition, material, lighting, and
camera study.

The visual thesis is:

> **A 2004 commuter line remembers the impossible late-game RPG that passed
> through it.**

The infrastructure must be credible before it becomes supernatural. The train,
rail town, wet road, power lines, old clockwork, and concrete should look like
they have ordinary municipal histories. Nightmare fantasy then enters through
enemy anatomy, impossible motion, deferred impacts, and the landscape failing
in specific places. It does not enter as a global VHS, posterization, or
backrooms filter.

No image-generation source is used or recommended by this document.

## Non-negotiable visual laws

1. **The opening is a place, not a movie screen.** The player can look freely
   around the carriage for the entire train ride and crash. Choreography is
   carried by sound, light passing across the windows, nearby passengers or
   abandoned seats, objects shifting, and events occurring around the player.
   Never lock the view down the playable road and call it a train.
2. **Normal must be allowed to look normal.** A quiet wall can simply be old
   concrete. A dark window can simply reflect the carriage. Constant
   distortion destroys the contrast needed for Watchfire, wounds, enemy
   manifestations, and the crash.
3. **The town has three mundane light families:** old fluorescent bone inside
   the train, dirty sodium amber on streets and platforms, and weak red signal
   lamps. Watchfire purple is the only impossible light family.
4. **Wet is patchy.** Asphalt has broken puddles and thin damp films, not a
   universal mirror. Ballast remains mostly rough and dark. Rail tops alone
   catch hard highlights while their sides remain oxidized.
5. **Fantasy is silhouetted before it is explained.** The player first reads a
   crown, blade, halo, service-tower spine, or impossible limb spacing. Fine
   surface noise comes second.
6. **General-audience horror comes from aftermath.** Use broken glass, torn
   timetables, scorched outlines, cracked ballast, empty shoes, ash, bent
   metal, and impossible body posture. The commercial apocalypse pack contains
   blood and body meshes; those are not part of this game's visual language.

## The six must-use references

### 1. A real piece of 2004: Helsinki Metro M100 interior

![Helsinki Metro M100 interior](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:Redirect/file/Helsinki_Metro_train_interior.jpg?width=960)

- **Page:** [Helsinki Metro train
  interior](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Helsinki_Metro_train_interior.jpg)
- **Creator/source:** Lussmu, own photograph transferred from Finnish
  Wikipedia, 8 April 2004.
- **License:** [CC BY 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/)
- **What matters:** orange moulded seats, gray floor, stainless rails,
  repetitive ceiling lights, windows, advertisements, and commuters make a
  completely specific early-2000s public interior. The aesthetic comes from
  ordinary manufactured objects sharing one cramped perspective, not from a
  filter.
- **Translation:** age the orange toward burnt rust and burgundy; shift the
  ceiling light toward old fluorescent ivory; leave metal neutral and
  scratched. Windows should be mostly black at night, intermittently crossed
  by poles, platform lights, and the player's reflection. Keep one or two
  intensely mundane details near the eye: a fabric seat seam, a route-map
  frame, a yellow door edge, or a scratched grab rail.
- **Camera:** seated-or-standing eye height inside the carriage, with nearby
  seat backs and poles providing parallax when the player looks around. The
  camera remains player-controlled. The scene passes outside the windows; the
  camera does not pan to show the future level.
- **Local mapping:**
  - carriage proportion study:
    `../asset/extracted/POLYGON - Western Pack - Art by Synty/6e17b4f6d557b41488d66ba9d108795d/asset`
    (`SM_Veh_Train_Carriage_01.fbx`);
  - crash exterior:
    `../asset/extracted/Low Poly Ultimate Pack/e08e8bccfef4eea49beba66e4243a268/asset`
    (`SM_Train_Speed_Derailment_Apocalypse.fbx`);
  - the playable interior should still be authored from modular pieces so the
    windows, seats, handrails, and free-look sightlines are intentional.

### 2. Pools of safety in a black rail world: Stettbach by night

![Stettbach railway station at night](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:Redirect/file/Stettbach_by_night.jpg?width=960)

- **Page:** [Stettbach by

- **What matters:** the station reads as a narrow sequence of useful light

  vanishing infrastructure create unease without deleting the identity of the

  nearer layers: a fence plus a pole, a wreck plus a lamp, or a doorway through

### 3. The hero prop: a broken pocketwatch scanned in 2005

![Broken pocketwatch](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:Redirect/file/Pocketwatch_-_Flickr_-_dvs.jpg?width=720)

- **Page:** [Pocketwatch — Flickr —

  vision.

### 5. Late-game enemy architecture: Balfron Tower concrete

![Balfron Tower concrete](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:Redirect/file/Balfron_Tower_concrete.jpg?width=960)

- **Page:** [Balfron Tower
  concrete](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Balfron_Tower_concrete.jpg)

## Supporting references with one precise job

### Wet road without cyberpunk

![Wet street in Bar, Montenegro](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:Redirect/file/Wet_street_%288458963823%29.jpg?width=960)

- [Wet street in Bar,
  Montenegro](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Wet_street_(8458963823).jpg),
  Alexandra Zakharova, 10 February 2013,
  [CC BY 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/).
- **Take:** dim local reflections, rough road still visible between wet patches,
  muted town scale.
- **Do not take:** saturated cyan/magenta signage, every surface reflecting, or
  a global blue-orange grade.
- **Production rule:** puddles occupy roughly 10–20% of a road segment. Their
  reflected hues are fluorescent ivory, weak sodium, signal red, and—in
  Watchfire only—deep purple.

### Time bending as accumulated direction, not blur soup

![Railway station light trails](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:Redirect/file/Light_trails_on_a_railway_station_%28Unsplash%29.jpg?width=960)

- [Light trails on a railway
  station](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Light_trails_on_a_railway_station_(Unsplash).jpg),
  Mike Wilson, 17 August 2016, a pre-license-change Unsplash photograph
  verified on Commons as [CC0
  1.0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/).
- **Take:** motion has an origin, a direction, a duration, and an endpoint.
- **Do not take:** the photograph's multiple bright colors. Chronosword trails
  are warm ivory/gray for ordinary motion and dark purple-lilac for time art.
- **Production rule:** smear only moving objects and only behind their actual
  velocity. The player hands, crosshair, watch rim, and stationary architecture
  stay crisp.

## Watchfire: the missing badass contrast

Watchfire should not feel like an overlay that makes the whole game sluggish.
The fantasy is that **the world has become too slow for the player**.

### Entry: 80–120 ms

- The watch snaps up before the screen treatment begins.
- The seconds hand violently accelerates backward and produces two or three
  readable analog-hand echoes.
- A single dark-violet pressure ripple travels outward from the left hand.
- Hostile sound loses its high end; the player's cloth, foot, dagger, and impact
  transients remain immediate.
- Enemy animation and projectile travel visibly collapse toward near-stillness
  within the entry beat. The player camera never eases into molasses.

### Active state

- Hostile motion is roughly one-tenth speed; player motion, hand animation,
  turning, attacks, jumping, and world-space damage text remain full cadence.
- Enemies lose a little saturation and contrast. The watch, hands, rail sparks,
  and hit confirmation retain normal contrast, visually separating actor time
  from world time.
- Hostile projectiles become readable objects with a thin history showing
  exactly where they came from. They do not become soft blobs.
- Dust, ticket scraps, glass chips, and rail sparks hang in the air near their
  origin. Walking through this suspended evidence sells mastery better than
  increasing bloom.
- Successful hits still get a sharp real-time contact frame, directional spark
  fan, enemy deformation, and one large world-space number. The slowed world
  must not soften the player's impact.
- The flame leans opposite hand acceleration like a real moving volume. Its hot
  core is small and pale lilac; most of its area is nearly black eggplant.
- A slight FOV expansion is acceptable. Full-screen radial blur, underwater
  refraction, and indiscriminate history blending are not.

### Exit: 100–160 ms

- History trails contract into their objects rather than simply fading.
- Suspended particles resume in their stored directions.
- Audio and contrast return with one hard mechanical tick.
- If an enemy dies during Watchfire, its final pose may hold for a fraction
  longer before gravity catches it, preserving the player's authored tableau.

The reference lesson from Bayonetta, SUPERHOT, and the user's “worm time”
comparison is functional rather than cosmetic: the hostile world becoming
slow only feels powerful when the player remains fast, precise, loud, and able
to rearrange the scene.

## Palette and exposure budget

The scene should read correctly in grayscale before accents are added.

| Function | Color | Share / rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Soot black | `#111512` | Main void, ceiling recesses, unlit windows |
| Rail midnight | `#182026` | Cool dark separation, never a blue wash |
| Wet tar | `#272B2B` | Road base; puddles dip darker |
| Old concrete | `#747166` | Midtone anchor, stained rather than yellow |
| Fluorescent bone | `#D4CCAE` | Interior/key readability, not pure white |
| Oxidized bronze | `#69452D` | Watch rim, rails, handled metal |
| Sodium ember | `#B2763F` | Sparse exterior pools |
| Wound rust | `#71281E` | Trauma, cracked maximum-time rim, signal history |
| Watchfire shadow | `#2A1035` | Most of the flame and time-art shadow |
| Watchfire core | `#B68BC7` | Tiny hottest region only |

Practical distribution:

- 70% soot, rail midnight, and wet tar;
- 20% concrete and fluorescent bone;
- 7% rust, sodium, and signal red;
- at most 3% Watchfire purple outside explicit time-art moments.

There is no teal-orange grade. There is no global purple fog.

## Material translation

### Train interior

- aged ivory painted ceiling, roughness `0.65–0.8`;
- burnt-rust vinyl/plastic seats, broad worn highlights but no metallic;
- stainless handrails, roughness `0.35–0.5`, fingerprints near grip height;
- dark rubber floor with lighter embedded grit and a few wet shoe marks;
- black windows with low-contrast interior reflections and intermittent
  exterior light travel.

### Road, ballast, and rail

- asphalt has two scales: broad repaired patches and fine aggregate;
- ballast stones are individually readable only near the player; at distance
  they become a rough charcoal mass;
- rail tops are smoother and brighter; rail webs and fasteners are oxidized;
- puddles sit in depressions and along kerbs, never uniformly across slopes;
- impact sparks inherit rail direction and throw a few hot particles along the
  track rather than making a circular fireworks burst.

### Concrete

- combine broad damp staining, edge chipping, and one fine aggregate normal;
- do not multiply a high-frequency grime texture over every face;
- silhouettes and values do most of the work at browser resolution;
- the same concrete family should connect station, road barrier, old housing,
  and boss armor so the past appears to be literally rising out of the town.

### Glass and paper

- train and café glass reflect before they transmit;
- broken glass is sparse, chunky, and catches one-frame glints;
- timetable paper, tickets, warnings, and route maps use dead ivory rather
  than screen white;
- paper scraps are ideal suspended Watchfire debris because they show both
  direction and stopped gravity without gore.

### Licensing boundary

- `asset.meta` entries in **Low Poly Ultimate Pack** and **POLYGON Western**
  explicitly report `licenseType: Store`. They are commercial Unity Asset
  Store content supplied by the user, not public-domain files. Raw sources must
  remain uncommitted, and an export may include them only under the user's
  valid entitlement and applicable store license.
- No standalone license notice was found beside the selected **White mage
  spells** textures during this audit. Treat them as entitlement-only Store
  content until the package license is confirmed.
- **OverCloud** was inspected and rejected for this pass: its root license says
  it is distributed per-seat through the Unity Asset Store and forbids source
  redistribution without consent. Some component code has permissive licenses,
  but the Unity system is not a clean Godot visual source.
- Kenney assets already in the repository are CC0 and safe to redistribute.

## One connected composition

The level should not be “road with props on both sides.” It should have a
three-act spatial silhouette:

1. **Carriage:** dense near-field frames, bone fluorescent light, black windows,
   ordinary detail, freedom to look.
2. **Wreck and town:** a torn train creates the first threshold; rails and power
   lines lead through alternating light islands; old housing blocks one side
   while a factory hall slowly becomes the horizon.
3. **Unfinished site:** the road and rail geometries converge, then break.
   Concrete forms rise where architecture and enemy can no longer be separated.

At any point, the player should be able to name what the place used to be. At
any encounter, the player should be able to name what impossible event is
happening to it.

## Fast implementation order

If there are only hours left, aesthetics improve fastest in this order:

1. Make the train a true enclosing free-look space with windows, seat/pole
   parallax, moving exterior lights, and no forced stare toward the town.
2. Replace the flat road composition with the wreck threshold, old block,
   factory horizon, power-line rhythm, and purposeful light islands.
3. Wire the local night-emission/specular atlases so windows, signals, metal,
   and glass stop sharing one inert material response.
4. Give the ground one coherent worn-asphalt/ballast family with patchy
   wetness, polished rail tops, and oxidized sides.
5. Rebuild Watchfire around player/world asymmetry: near-frozen hostiles, crisp
   full-speed player, velocity-correct trails, suspended debris, hard impact.
6. Build every major enemy as a black silhouette first, using architectural
   mass and one readable weapon or halo before adding surface detail.
